# Restaurant Websites: product journeys

## Product intent

Make a restaurant's customer site feel like its own business, while keeping onboarding and content maintenance repeatable across restaurants. The following journeys describe the product structure observed in source. They are design explanations, not app screenshots or usability-study results.

| Person | Primary task | Design emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guest | Browse dishes, prices, availability, and restaurant information. | A readable mobile menu and a clear language choice. |
| Owner | Keep sections, dishes, sizes, photos, hours, and branding current. | Direct controls close to the content being changed. |
| Operator | Create and support restaurant accounts and domains. | An explicit restaurant identity, status, and support context. |

## Proposed portfolio storyboard

1. **Bring a restaurant onto the service.** Show a concept frame for tenant setup and owner access; use synthetic content.
2. **Make it recognizably theirs.** Show brand/theme controls beside a clearly labelled concept of the guest site.
3. **Keep the menu current.** Show the idea of section management, quick item entry, and availability controls.
4. **Meet the guest where they are.** Show English/Arabic presentation and a narrow-screen menu concept.

Any future illustrated frames must say **concept illustration**, unless an actual screenshot is captured and its provenance and scope are reviewed. Current source does not provide a portfolio usability measurement.

## Design decisions worth explaining

Menu editing, customer browsing, and operator support are different tasks. Give them distinct presentations and make the active restaurant context visible. Explain bilingual layout, integer price storage, and tenant-specific branding as product-engineering decisions.

Ordering, payments, and delivery screens belong in a separately labelled future-concept section. Their inclusion in a storyboard must not suggest current implementation.
